What do the 4 nearest neighbours of an image share with it?
==========================================================================
Every image of the five pooled evaluation sets, 4,000 in total, by cosine distance in
each representation. A neighbour is never the query itself. Chance is the rate a
randomly drawn neighbour would achieve given the set sizes, which is well above zero.

Corpus and generator are separated deliberately. Asking only whether a neighbour comes
from the query's own evaluation set confounds the two: the CIFAKE sets differ from the
ImageNet sets in subject matter as well as in origin, so a representation reading pure
content would score high on that question for the wrong reason. The third column asks
the question that is not confounded, restricted to the three ImageNet-based sets, which
share their photographic content and differ only in which generator produced them.

representation         same corpus   same class   same generator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
chance                       0.500        0.500            0.375
frozen CLIP                  0.965        0.701            0.482
DSF-Net fused                0.732        0.736            0.500

The third column is the one that carries the mechanism claim. Within the ImageNet
corpus, where every set shows the same kind of photograph and the only thing that
changes is the generator, chance is 0.375.

  frozen CLIP      generator agreement 0.482, which is +0.108 against chance
  DSF-Net fused    generator agreement 0.500, which is +0.126 against chance

DSF-Net fused groups images by their generator more strongly than frozen CLIP does.

Read the first column against the third. Both representations put same-corpus
images together far above chance, but that column cannot distinguish a space
organised by origin from one organised by subject matter, because in these data
the two travel together. The third column separates them, and it is much closer
to chance for both, which is the honest form of the claim: neither representation
groups images by generator anywhere near as strongly as the raw set-membership
number would suggest.
