Can a trivial statistic separate the classes in each evaluation set?
==========================================================================
Logistic regression on twelve per-image summary statistics, five-fold cross
validated. No pixel positions, no learned features, no container metadata: every
image is already a 32x32 array, so format and dimension cues cannot survive.

set                                  balanced accuracy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A: CIFAKE test (in distribution)                0.6730
C: ImageNet vs ADM                              0.5860
C: ImageNet vs BigGAN                           0.6330
C: ImageNet vs GLIDE                            0.6330
C: ImageNet vs Midjourney                       0.5900
B: ImageNet vs SD15                             0.6180
C: ImageNet vs VQDM                             0.7470
C: ImageNet vs Wukong                           0.6990

Highest is 0.7470 on C: ImageNet vs VQDM.

What matters for the decomposition is the comparison between A and B, since their
difference is the corpus term. Those two screen at 0.6730 and 0.6180.
The trivial-separability gap between them is 5.50 points, against measured
corpus terms of 19.70 to 28.71 points depending on the detector.

A gap of this size is not negligible. At most a few points of the corpus term
could be reproduced by a model reading nothing but summary statistics, so the
majority of it is not trivially separable, but the fraction that is must be
reported rather than assumed away.

Note also the absolute levels. Both sets are partly separable by twelve statistics,
CIFAKE at 0.6730 and the ImageNet pairing at 0.6180.
That is a property of these benchmarks worth stating on its own: a detector reporting
high accuracy on CIFAKE is beating a baseline well above chance, not one at 0.50.
